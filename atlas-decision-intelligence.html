ATLAS · PepsiCo UXD

I championed bringing UX in earlier, so teams reach the right concept before the rework.

On ATLAS, an AI negotiation tool for PepsiCo’s negotiation leaders, a UX-led team prototyped and tested a working concept in three weeks. Other teams now ask for the same approach.

The shift

The usual way

UX joins after the concept is set.

Ideas get tested late, so every wrong turn becomes rework.

What I championed

UX close to the problem, from the start.

Prototype and test early with AI tools, so the team learns what works before the build.

The system I set up

As UX lead, I set how a team of six designers worked together, and spent 75–90% of my time on ATLAS.

Shared code

I proposed working in GitHub with our prototyping tools, mentored another designer on it, and owned merging everyone’s work.

Testing without users

Proxy users plus three synthetic personas built from the jobs to be done.

Direction AI can follow

Design direction written so AI tools produce a polished, human-crafted UI.

A weekly loop

Build, measure, learn, every week, with a shared prototype link everyone could open.

Bringing the team along

At first, everyone said GitHub was too technical, and that I was too technical. I kept it a conversation about value, not tools.

I showed it by doing the work, and started with the people who were curious. Once they saw it working, the rest of the team came on board.

Solving the right problem

In three weeks, we had a prototype aimed at the right problem, tested by people who know the negotiation space, before any build began.

Each persona tested a different adoption risk

Christina · strategy owner

Can the strategy owner trust it?

Kam · field negotiator

Can the field use it in the room?

Laura · governance

Can governance control it?

We couldn’t test with CNOs themselves. Proxy users with history in the space stood in, and the personas flagged what to check with CNOs.

The product it produced

ATLAS moved from “just more data” to a negotiation intelligence hub: a question to a defensible answer in under 60 seconds.

The product bet: negotiators don’t need more data, they need to know what to do next. So ATLAS leads with what needs attention, and builds the strategy from there.

Outcome

3 weeks → a tested concept

I presented ATLAS and the approach to the SVP of Product and the VP of UX. Since then, I’ve used a version of this sprint and the synthetic personas on other projects.

Other teams now ask for rapid POCs like this, including a supply chain team, bringing me in early to prototype alongside product, engineering and transformation. GitHub is on its way to becoming our standard.

Still refining: handoffs between AI tools, and bias in proxy feedback from teams close to the work.

What I took forward

When UX is closer to the problem, teams reach the right concept faster.

Earlier prototyping and testing means less rework later. I now push to bring UX in at the start of AI projects, with prototypes, personas and shared code.
