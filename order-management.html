Order Management · PepsiCo

Replacing email, fax and PDF orders with one standardized path.

As primary UX designer, I’m designing a web application where large orders are submitted, validated and tracked in one place, so they can finally be automated and followed.

Role
Primary UX designer
Timeline
May 2025 – present
Team
Product, UX, engineering, business stakeholders
Status
In delivery

In 30 seconds

Problem

Vendors sent large orders by email, phone, fax and PDF, so nothing could be automated or tracked.

Insight

The problem wasn’t the methods. It was having no central system.

Approach

Standardize submission while keeping room for different customer needs.

Evidence

Pre-launch research: 33% of orders submitted manually; 43% needed manual intervention.

The situation

Every order arrived in a different shape.

Each format needed manual interpretation and data entry. Sales reps and plants worked in silos, so miscommunication and delays were common, and hours went to work that could be automated. Among Frito-Lay orders, half were submitted manually.

The experience model

Design principles

Standard where it helps, flexible where customers differ.

Standardize the structure of an order so it can be automated. Keep the lifecycle visible to everyone involved. Move communication between sales reps and plants into the order itself instead of side channels.

Demo videos and detailed workflow screens are confidential. Ask for a walkthrough.
